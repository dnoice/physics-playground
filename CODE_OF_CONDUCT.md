# Code of conduct

Physics Playground exists so that anyone can play with physics, learn from
it and help build it. We want everyone who takes part, from first-time
visitors to long-time contributors, to feel welcome, safe and respected,
whatever their age, background, identity, ability or experience.

## What we hope for

- **Be kind and patient.** Many people here are learning, and some are
  learning physics, programming or English for the first time.
- **Welcome questions.** No question is too basic. A good answer helps the
  next person who wonders the same thing.
- **Assume good intent.** Ask before you conclude, and give people the
  chance to explain or put something right.
- **Critique the work, not the person.** Be specific about what could be
  better, and say what is good too.
- **Give credit.** Name the people and sources whose work you build on.
- **Own your mistakes.** Apologise, learn and carry on. Everyone gets things
  wrong sometimes.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation or threats of any kind.
- Insults, slurs, or demeaning jokes about who someone is.
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Repeatedly derailing discussions, or trolling.
- Encouraging or excusing any of the above.

## Where it applies

This code applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, reviews,
commit messages and any discussion the project hosts. It also applies when
someone represents the project elsewhere.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour worries you, or you are unsure whether it crosses a
line, please tell the maintainer, Dennis Smaltz (`dnoice` on GitHub),
privately:

- If the maintainer's [GitHub profile](https://github.com/dnoice) lists a
  way to get in touch, use it.
- Otherwise, open an issue titled "Private conduct report" containing
  **no details**. The maintainer will reply with a private way to talk.

Reports are kept confidential. Only the people who need to know to deal with
a report will see it.

## What happens next

The maintainer aims to acknowledge a report within a few days, look into it
fairly, and tell the reporter what was decided. The response depends on
what happened and may be:

- a private word about the behaviour and its effect;
- a written warning;
- a temporary break from the project's spaces;
- a permanent ban, for serious or repeated harm.

No one who reports a concern in good faith will face retaliation for it. If
a concern involves the maintainer, raise it with GitHub through the
**Report** option on the content or the user's profile.

Thank you for helping keep this a place where curiosity is safe.
